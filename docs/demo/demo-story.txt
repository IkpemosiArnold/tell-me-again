My name is Mama Efe, and I was born in Warri in 1951. Our house had a zinc roof that sang whenever it rained. My father, Papa Okoro, fished on the river every morning before the sun came up. My mother sold pepper soup at Ogbe market, and everybody in that market knew her laugh before they saw her face.

In 1960, on Independence Day, we walked to the town square. I was nine years old. I remember the green and white flag going up, and an old man next to me crying, and I did not understand why a grown man would cry at a flag. My mother told me, you will understand when you are older. She was right.

The next year I started at Saint Mary's School. My best friend was a girl called Tari. We raced each other home every day, and the loser carried both school bags. I lost more than I won, but I will deny it if you ask Tari.

When I finished school in 1969, I went to Benin City to train as a nurse. That was where I met your grandfather, Daniel. He came to the hospital with a broken arm from a football match, and he asked me the same question every day for two weeks: what time do you close? I married him in 1974, in the church on Airport Road.

We moved to Lagos in 1978 because Daniel got work at the port in Apapa. Lagos was loud. Too loud. I missed the river. But we built a life, and your father was born in Surulere in 1980.

If you want to know one thing about me, know this. I never let anybody leave my house hungry. Even when money no dey, there was always rice, and there was always somebody's child eating it.
